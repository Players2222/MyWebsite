How I Work

I learn best by getting my hands dirty. I built a Scheme interpreter in C because I wanted to understand how code actually executes at memory level, and I put together a plastic granulator because I wanted to see how recycling machinery worked step-by-step. It usually means failing a few times before getting it right, which is half the fun.

Right now, I work with Professor Martha-Elizabeth Baylor studying light-sensitive plastics for optofluidic chips. Before this, I designed optics outreach demos for classrooms. If a project involves sitting at a computer and working at a lab bench in the same afternoon, I’m into it.

Research Experience

Integrated Optofluidic Devices

Sep 2026 – Present

Research Assistant | Carleton College | Prof. Martha-Elizabeth Baylor

Optofluidic chips pair liquid micro-channels directly with optical light guides. I study the light-sensitive plastics (photopolymers) used to make them. My team and I build test setups to measure refractive index changes, stiffness, and optical loss across different polymer recipes.

The practical goal: build a clear material catalog so researchers can pick the right polymer—whether that's a flexible rubbery blend for wearable sensors or a durable glass-like blend for industrial tools.
